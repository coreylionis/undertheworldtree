These notes are from a talk given by Yaping Yang at the University of Melbourne during semester 1, 2026. The talk series was designed to introduce master's students to research topics in representation theory. Her talk was about the (geometric) representation theory of quantum groups.  

Interest in quantum groups in the 80's came from the discovery of quantum integrable systems such as the Yang-Baxter equations 
$$
R_{12}R_{13}R_{12} = R_{23}R_{12}R_{23}
$$
relating maps $R_{ij}: V^{\otimes 2} \to V^{\otimes 2}$. The Yang-Baxter equation can be thought as the algebraic side of the Reidemeister relation

under a topological field theory correspondence, so that understanding its algebra translates back to topological and geometric data. Drinfeld and Jimbo developed the algebraic formalism unifying these systems by *deforming* a Hopf algebra. 

An example is enough to illustrate the general idea. THEN ALSO INCLUDE A GENERAL LIE GROUP DEFINITION AND COMPARE. For the Lie algebra $ \frr{g} = \frr{sl}_2 $  and its standard triple of generators 
$$
h = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad e = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad f = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
$$
we first pass to the universal enveloping algebra 
$$
\cal{U}(\frr{g}) = \bigl \langle h, e, f \ | \ [h, e] = 2e, [h,f] = -2f, [e, f] = h  \bigr \rangle
$$
and define a $q$-deformation. Where the enveloping algebra was a $\bb{C}$-algebra, the $q$-deformation becomes a $\bb{C}(q)$-algebra $U_q(\frr{sl}_2)$ having four generators $E, F, K^{\pm}$ and relations 
$$
KEK^{-1} = q^2E, \quad KFK^{-1} = q^{-2}F, \quad [E, F] = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}. 
$$
With $q = e^{\frac{\hbar}{2}}$ and $K = \text{exp}(\frac{\hbar}{2}h)$ the corresponding matrix exponential, taking the limit $\hbar \to 0$ recovers $\frr{sl}_2$. The quantum deformation can therefore be thought of as a deformation of $\frr{sl}_2$ in one 'direction'. 

#### Hopf algebra structure
The deformed algebra has a nontrivial Hopf algebra structure with comultiplication $\Delta : \cal{U}_q(\frr{g}) \to \cal{U}_q(\frr{g})^{\otimes 2}$ given by
$$
E \mapsto E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E, \quad F \mapsto F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F, \quad K \mapsto K \otimes K 
$$
such that $\Delta \neq \Delta^{\text{op}}$. The Yang-Baxter $\cal{R}$-matrices *intertwine* the multiplications, $R\Delta R^{-1} = \Delta^{\text{op}}$.

### Representations of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$
CONTEXT 
For a generic choice of complex parameter $q$,one has 
$$
\cal{U}_q(\frr{g}) \simeq \cal{U}(\frr{g})\otimes \bb{C}(q).
$$
This means that often representations of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ intertwine $\frr{g}$-actions with multiplication by $q$.  

#### Category $\cal{O}$
Category $\cal{O}$ is an abelian enlargment of the category of Verma modules. The integral subcategory $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}$ consists of modules built using the integral weights $P$ of the Lie algebra $\frr{g}$. 


Working with $\frr{sl}_2$ for simplicity, for each weight $\mu \in P$ let $V_{\mu}$ be the finite-dimensional weight module which is a quotient of the weight-$\mu$ Verma module. This means that $V_\mu$ is generated by a single highest-weight vector $v$ such that 
$$
Kv = q^{\langle \mu, \alpha^\vee \rangle} v,
$$
where $\alpha^\vee$ is the simple dominant coroot of $\frr{sl}_2$.

The integral subcategory $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}(\frr{sl}_2)$ consists of all $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$-modules where 

- The action of $K$ on $V$ is diagonalisable, with eigenspace decomposition 
$$
\begin{equation*} V = \bigoplus_{\mu \in P} V_{\mu}, \end{equation*}
$$

- For all $v \in V$, $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ is finite-dimensional.


<u>**Theorem:**</u> The simple objects of $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}$ are the weight modules $L(w)$ with *regular* dominant integral weights, 
$$
L(w) = V_w, w \in \bb{Z}_{> 0}.
$$ 
<br />
With this phrasing, the theorem generalises to arbitrary $\frr{g}$ after correctly identifying the weight lattice. Since $P$ denotes the dominant integral weights, we also write $P^+$ for the set of regular weights in $P$.

<u>**Theorem:**</u> For all modules $V \in \cal{O}_{\text{int}}$, 
$$
\text{ch}(V) = \sum_{\mu \in P} \text{dim}(V_\mu) e^{\mu} \in \bb{Z}[P],
$$
where $\text{ch}$ sends $V$ to its character and $e^\mu$ is the idempotent associated with $V_\mu$. The character $\text{ch}(L(w))$ is determined by a Weyl character formula.

In the $\frr{sl}_2$ case, $L(w) = \text{Span}\{X_{w-2r} \ | \ 0 \leq r \leq w \}$ and $E$, $F$ act by raising/lowering: 
$$
F^rX_w = X_{w-2r}, \quad E^rX_{w-2r} = X_w.
$$
Of course, $EX_w = 0$. 

#### Integral form
GEOMETRIC INTERPRETATIONS
Let $\cal{A} = \bb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$, and let 
$$
[s]_q = \frac{q^s - q^{-s}}{q - q^{-1}},
$$
with $s \in \bb{N}$. This formula is called the $q$-binomial. Its first values are
$$
[1]_q = 1, \quad [2]_q = q + q^{-1}, \quad [3]_q = q^2 + 1 + q^{-2}. 
$$
Using this formula we define the $q$-factorial 
$$
[n]_q\! = [n]_q \cdot \dots \cdot [1]_q.
$$ 
The **divided power quantum algebra** is the $\cal{A}$-subalgebra of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ generated by the elements 
$$
E^{(N)} = \frac{E^N}{[N]_q\!}, \quad F^{(N)} = \frac{F^N}{[N]_q\!}, \quad K^{\pm}
$$
for $N \in \bb{N}$. Lusztig's quantum algebra for the $\ell$th root of unity $\eps$ is defined to be 
$$
\cal{U}_{\eps}(\frr{g}) = \cal{U}_{\cal{A}} \otimes_{\bb{Z}[q^{\pm}]} \bb{C}_{\eps},
$$
where the notation $\bb{C}_{\eps}$ means we make $\bb{C}$ into an $\cal{A}$-module with $q$ acting by $\eps$. 

The parameter $\eps$ reduces the generating set of dividing powers to a finite set $E, F, K^{\pm}, E^{(\ell)}, F^{(\ell)}$. 
As an example, $\eps^3 = 1$ gives 
$$
\eps + \eps^2 = -1 \implies E^{(2)} = -E^2
$$
so that the denominator disappears. 

Defining $L(w)$ in the same way for $\cal{U}_{\eps}(\frr{sl}_2)$ as for $\cal{U}_q(\frr{sl}_2)$, the eigenvalues are all given in terms of $\eps$: 
$$
\begin{aligned}
KX_{w - 2r} &= \eps^{w-2r}X_{w - 2r}, \\ 
FX_{w-2r} &= [r+1]_{\eps}X_{w - 2r - 2}, \\ 
EX_{w - 2r} &= [w - r + 1]_{\eps} X_{w-2r + 2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This module is no longer irreducible, but the quotient $L(w)' = L(w)/ \text{Span}\{X_\ell, X_{-\ell}\}$ replaces it.

<u>**Lusztig's Theorem:**</u> Let $w \in P^+$. Then the character of $L(w)'$ is given by the formula 
$$
\text{ch}[L(w)'] = \sum_{y \in P^+} \eta_{w, y} P_{w, y}(1) \text{ch}[L(w)],
$$
where $P_{w, y}$ is the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial with parameters $w, y$ and $\eta_{w,y} \in \{\pm 1\}$.  

There is also a third variant quantum group, the **de Concini-Kac** quantum group $\cal{U}^{DK}_{\eps}(\frr{g})$. 

### Geometric representations
COMPARE TO LANGLANDS CONTEXT, GEOMETRIC QUANTIZATION
The positive part $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})^+$ of the quantum eveloping algebra $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ can be thought of as a subalgebra of a Ringel-Hall algebra. This Lusztig-Ringel-Hall construction specifies the *canonical basis* of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$, and connects the representation theory of the algebra with the Dynkin quiver of $\frr{g}$.
