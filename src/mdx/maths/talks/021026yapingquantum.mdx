These notes are from a talk given by Yaping Yang at the University of Melbourne during semester 1, 2026. Her talk was an introduction to the (geometric) representation theory of quantum groups, demonstrating results and definitions for quantum-$\frr{sl}_2$.  
{/* excerpt end */}

Interest in quantum groups in the 1980s came from the discovery of quantum integrable systems such as the Yang-Baxter equations 
$$
R_{23}R_{13}R_{12} = R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}
$$
relating maps of vector spaces $R_{ij}: V^{\otimes 2} \to V^{\otimes 2}$. The Yang-Baxter equation can be thought as the algebraic side of the Reidemeister relation

under a topological field theory correspondence. As a result, understanding the algebra of the Yang-Baxter equation translates into topological and geometric data. Drinfeld and Jimbo developed the algebraic formalism unifying these systems by *deforming* the universal enveloping algebra of a semisimple Lie algebra. 

For the Lie algebra $ \frr{g} = \frr{sl}_2 $ and its standard triple of generators 
$$
h = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad e = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad f = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
$$
the universal enveloping algebra is 
$$
\cal{U}(\frr{g}) = \bigl \langle h, e, f \ | \ [h, e] = 2e, [h,f] = -2f, [e, f] = h  \bigr \rangle.
$$
The enveloping algebra is a $\bb{C}$-algebra, and $q$-deformation creates a related $\bb{C}(q)$-algebra $U_q(\frr{sl}_2)$ having four generators $E, F, K^{\pm}$ and relations 
$$
KEK^{-1} = q^2E, \quad KFK^{-1} = q^{-2}F, \quad [E, F] = \frac{K - K^{-1}}{q - q^{-1}}. 
$$
We can specialise parameters to see the relation to $\cal{U}(\frr{sl}_2)$: letting $q = e^{\frac{\hbar}{2}}$, $E = \text{exp}(\frac{\hbar}{2}e)$, $F = \text{exp}(\frac{\hbar}{2}f)$ and $K = \text{exp}(\frac{\hbar}{2}h)$ the corresponding matrix exponentials, taking the limit $\hbar \to 0$ recovers $\frr{sl}_2$. 

The quantum deformation can therefore be thought of as a deformation of $\frr{sl}_2$ in one 'direction'. The general definition for a semisimple Lie algebra/Kac-Moody group can be found [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_group#Drinfeld%E2%80%93Jimbo_type_quantum_groups).


#### Hopf algebra structure
The deformed algebra carries a Hopf algebra structure with the comultiplication map <br />
$\Delta : \cal{U}_q(\frr{g}) \to \cal{U}_q(\frr{g})^{\otimes 2}$ defined by 
$$
E \mapsto E \otimes K + 1 \otimes E, \quad F \mapsto F \otimes 1 + K^{-1} \otimes F, \quad K \mapsto K \otimes K. 
$$
The Hopf algebras coming from these $q$-deformations have the special property that $\Delta \neq \Delta^{\text{op}}$. Yang-Baxter $\cal{R}$-matrices<sup><a href="#footnote1" className="internal-link" style={{fontWeight:"700"}}>1</a></sup> *intertwine* the multiplications, $R\Delta R^{-1} = \Delta^{\text{op}}$.


### Representations of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$
For a generic choice of complex parameter $q$, one has 
$$
\cal{U}_q(\frr{g}) \simeq \cal{U}(\frr{g})\otimes \bb{C}(q).
$$
In these cases, representations of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ intertwine $\frr{g}$-actions with multiplication by $q$.  


#### Category $\cal{O}$
Category $\cal{O}$ is an abelian category containing all finite semisimple modules and all Verma modules. The integral subcategory $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}$ consists of modules built using the integral weights $P$ of the Lie algebra $\frr{g}$. 

Working with $\frr{sl}_2$ for simplicity, for each weight $\mu \in P$ let $V_{\mu}$ be the finite-dimensional weight module which is a quotient of the weight-$\mu$ Verma module. This means that $V_\mu$ is generated by a single highest-weight vector $v$ such that 
$$
Kv = q^{\langle \mu, \alpha^\vee \rangle} v,
$$
where $\alpha^\vee$ is the simple dominant coroot of $\frr{sl}_2$.

The integral subcategory $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}(\frr{sl}_2)$ consists of all $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$-modules where 

- The action of $K$ on $V$ is diagonalisable, with eigenspace decomposition 
$$
\begin{equation*} V = \bigoplus_{\mu \in P} V_{\mu}, \end{equation*}
$$

- For all $v \in V$, $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ is finite-dimensional.

<u style={{fontSize:"calc(1.1*var(--base-font-size))"}}>**Theorem:**</u> The simple objects of $\cal{O}_{\text{int}}$ are the weight modules $L(w)$ with *regular* dominant integral weights, 
$$
L(w) = V_w, w \in \bb{Z}_{> 0}.
$$ 
<br />
With this phrasing, the theorem generalises to arbitrary $\frr{g}$ after correctly identifying the weight lattice. Since $P$ denotes the dominant integral weights, we also write $P^+$ for the set of regular weights in $P$.

<u style={{fontSize:"calc(1.1*var(--base-font-size))"}}>**Theorem:**</u> For all modules $V \in \cal{O}_{\text{int}}$, 
$$
\text{ch}(V) = \sum_{\mu \in P} \text{dim}(V_\mu) e^{\mu} \in \bb{Z}[P],
$$
where $\text{ch}$ sends $V$ to its character and $e^\mu$ is the idempotent associated with $V_\mu$. The character $\text{ch}(L(w))$ is determined by a Weyl character formula.

In the $\frr{sl}_2$ case, $L(w) = \text{Span}\{X_{w-2r} \ | \ 0 \leq r \leq w \}$ and $E$, $F$ act by raising/lowering: 
$$
F^rX_w = X_{w-2r}, \quad E^rX_{w-2r} = X_w.
$$
Of course, $EX_w = 0$. 


#### Integral form
Let $\cal{A} = \bb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$, and let 
$$
[s]_q = \frac{q^s - q^{-s}}{q - q^{-1}},
$$
with $s \in \bb{N}$. This formula is called the $q$-binomial. Its first values are
$$
[1]_q = 1, \quad [2]_q = q + q^{-1}, \quad [3]_q = q^2 + 1 + q^{-2}. 
$$
Using this formula we define the $q$-factorial 
$$
[n]_q\! = [n]_q \cdot \dots \cdot [1]_q.
$$ 
The **divided power quantum algebra** is the $\cal{A}$-subalgebra of $\cal{U}_q(\frr{g})$ generated by the elements 
$$
E^{(N)} = \frac{E^N}{[N]_q\!}, \quad F^{(N)} = \frac{F^N}{[N]_q\!}, \quad K^{\pm}
$$
for $N \in \bb{N}$. **Lusztig's quantum algebra** for the $\ell$th root of unity $\eps$ is defined to be 
$$
\cal{U}_{\eps}(\frr{g}) = \cal{U}_{\cal{A}} \otimes_{\bb{Z}[q^{\pm}]} \bb{C}_{\eps},
$$
where the notation $\bb{C}_{\eps}$ means we make $\bb{C}$ into an $\cal{A}$-module with $q$ acting by $\eps$. This is distinct from the more intuitive  <a href="#ref6" className="internal-link">de Concini-Kac</a> quantum group $\cal{U}^{DK}_{\eps}(\frr{g}) = \cal{U}_q(\frr{g})/(q-\eps)$. 

The parameter $\eps$ reduces the generating set of dividing powers to a finite set $E, F, K^{\pm}, E^{(\ell)}, F^{(\ell)}$. 
As an example, $\eps^3 = 1$ gives 
$$
\eps + \eps^2 = -1 \implies E^{(2)} = -E^2
$$
so that the denominator disappears. 

Defining $L(w)$ in the same way for $\cal{U}_{\eps}(\frr{sl}_2)$ as for $\cal{U}_q(\frr{sl}_2)$, the eigenvalues are all given in terms of $\eps$: 
$$
\begin{aligned}
KX_{w - 2r} &= \eps^{w-2r}X_{w - 2r}, \\ 
FX_{w-2r} &= [r+1]_{\eps}X_{w - 2r - 2}, \\ 
EX_{w - 2r} &= [w - r + 1]_{\eps} X_{w-2r + 2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This module is no longer irreducible, but the quotient $L(w)' = L(w)/ \text{Span}\{X_\ell, X_{-\ell}\}$ replaces it.

<u style={{fontSize:"calc(1.1*var(--base-font-size))"}}>**Lusztig's Theorem** [KL91](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792891000041):</u> Let $w \in P^+$. Then the character of $L(w)'$ is given by the formula 
$$
\text{ch}[L(w)'] = \sum_{y \in P^+} \eta_{w, y} P_{w, y}(1) \text{ch}[L(w)],
$$
where $P_{w, y}$ is the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial with parameters $w, y$ and $\eta_{w,y} \in \{\pm 1\}$....
